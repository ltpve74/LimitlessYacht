The breakthrough landed: indexation broke through. When Report #6 shipped, the new pages were live but invisible to Google — 4 pages indexed, the new ones at literal zero. Twelve days after we finally submitted the sitemap (10 Sep), Google has crawled it (Success · 48 URLs discovered) and indexed pages have jumped from 4 to 19. Seven of the new pages are now in the index and pulling impressions — including the first genuine non-brand charter queries we've ever seen. The engine we built has started turning over.
The catch: it's early, and it's off-season. The new pages are indexed but ranking on pages 3–9 (they're brand new, with little authority yet), so they earn impressions but almost no clicks so far — that's the next hurdle. Meanwhile raw clicks dipped this month, which is seasonal: charter search demand falls after the August peak, and every market softened (Ireland, the top market, went 13 → 3). Read this report for the structural wins — indexation and the arrival of real-market queries — not the headline click count.

What this means & what to do next

Where we are: the strategy has cleared its first real gate. The content shipped (Report #6), the sitemap was submitted, and Google has now indexed 19 pages with 7 new ones live in search and drawing their first impressions — including genuine, non-brand charter queries that never appeared before. That's the leading indicator we were waiting for.

The next job changes from "get indexed" to "get ranking." The new pages sit on pages 3–9. Moving them up is about authority and relevance, not indexing:

  • Let the 29 queued pages finish indexing — they'll clear over the coming weeks. Optionally nudge the priority ones (day-charter, the-Maiora, Cabrera, Es Trenc) with "Request Indexing" if Google's daily quota allows.
  • Prioritise /day-charter-mallorca/ — it's a genuine commercial query (25 impr) sitting at position 42.9. The best single target for on-page tightening + internal links from the homepage and destination pages.
  • Earn a few external signals. A handful of quality links / directory listings (charter directories, Club de Mar, tourism sites) would lift the whole new-page set out of the "discovered, not indexed" queue faster and help them rank.
  • Keep leaning on the real markets — Ireland, UK, US, Germany. Nothing here suggests changing the market focus.

When to judge it: next check ~mid-October (Report #8). By then more of the 29 should be indexed, and we'll see whether the new pages start climbing and converting impressions into clicks. That's the number that will prove the whole plan.
